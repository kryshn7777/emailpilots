# COMMON Site Brief — Part 07: Conversion, Copy & Scope
> Used in: Phase 1 (copy plan, conversion strategy) and Phase 5 (conversion integration). Copy rules apply to every phase that writes visitor-facing text.

## Conversion
The website is not an art installation. The visitor must understand:
1. What COMMON is.
2. Who it is for.
3. Why it matters.
4. Why it is different.
5. What they can do next.

Do not hide this behind excessive exploration — product clarity must coexist with spectacle. The main CTA is obvious, and its language reflects the **actual product action** (from the repo's real product flow), not generic "Learn More." Never invent pricing, guarantees, features, testimonials, claims, or statistics.

## Copy rules
No generic marketing copy. Banned register (and anything like it): "Revolutionize your workflow" · "Unlock your potential" · "Next-generation solutions" · "Powerful tools for modern teams."

Write specific copy derived from the actual product. Prefer: **short, confident, concrete, memorable.** Let typography and visuals communicate what copy doesn't need to explain.

## Scope / size constraint
Do NOT build a massive website. Avoid: dozens of sections · dozens of pages · giant feature lists · redundant scenes · enormous navigation systems · excessive copy · endless scrolling · unnecessary blog-like sections.

Think: **one exceptional journey.** One primary landing page · a few supporting utility pages only if genuinely required · one primary interactive experience · concise product information · clear conversion. The visitor should finish feeling they experienced something complete — not that they wandered a website for 12 minutes. A highly polished 60–120 second core experience beats a mediocre 8-minute one; longer exploration exists only as optional depth.

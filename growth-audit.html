01

Executive Summary

The premise of the original brief doesn't match the codebase. The request template was written for an Android wellness app (Play Store ASO, Calm/Headspace comps, meditation-style habit loops). This repository is Email Pilots: an Electron desktop app that sends personal outreach and follow-up email over SMTP and Microsoft Graph. Rather than force a mismatched framework onto the product, this audit re-runs the same rigor — positioning, activation, retention, monetization, growth loops, ICE-ranked roadmap — against what's actually here.

The product itself is in better shape than the "pre-revenue" framing suggests: multi-account sending, drip sequences that auto-stop on reply, a scheduler, shared suppression/bounce handling, AI-assisted reply classification, and a genuinely differentiated fully on-device AI assistant (writing help, personalization, spam advisory — zero cloud calls). That's a real product with a real edge.

Headline finding

The single biggest revenue bottleneck isn't a UX or pricing problem — it's that there is no way to pay. landing/src/config.ts defines a real plan ($2.9/wk, 7-day trial) but CHECKOUT_URL is a placeholder ('#pricing') with a code comment inviting a Stripe/Lemon Squeezy/Gumroad link that was never added. Every other recommendation in this report is downstream of fixing that first.

Second-order finding: there is no analytics or telemetry anywhere in the app (no PostHog, Mixpanel, Amplitude, or equivalent). That means every retention, activation, and funnel number quoted below is reasoned from code inspection and industry benchmarks — not live product data — because none exists yet. Standing up basic, privacy-respecting telemetry is itself a top recommendation, not an afterthought.

Biggest Revenue Bottlenecks

  1. No payment processor wired (Critical). CHECKOUT_URL = '#pricing' in landing/src/config.ts is a dead link. Every trial, every conversion optimization, every pricing experiment is moot until this exists.
  2. Freemium tier gating built but not enforced (Critical). src/ai/modelCatalog.ts defines AiTier = 'free' | 'first-class' with two real models (LFM2.5-350M vs Qwen3-4B), but per its own code comment and AUDIT-freemium.md, entitlement enforcement is sub-project #7 and unbuilt — both models are free to everyone today. The one feature engineered to justify a premium tier currently generates zero upgrade pressure.
  3. No trial-to-paid trigger (High). Sub-project #9 ("trial trigger") is named in the freemium audit doc but not implemented. There's no in-app moment that says "you're out of trial, pay now."
  4. Zero visibility into the funnel (High). Without telemetry, nobody knows where users drop off between install → first send → trial → paid. Every fix downstream of this section is currently a hypothesis, not a measurement.
  5. Weekly-only billing (Medium). A single $2.9/wk plan with no annual option forgoes the well-documented conversion lift of tiered/anchored pricing and annual-plan cash-flow benefits (see §12).

04

Critical UX Problems

The activation checklist vanishes forever

src/onboarding.ts shows a 3-step checklist (connect → recipient → start) that permanently disappears the moment totalSent > 0. That's clean for a first send, but it means the app has zero persistent "what's next" guidance after activation — no cross-sell into AI features, sequences, or domain-auth setup. A user who activates minimally never gets nudged toward the features that would make them stickier or upgrade-worthy.

Notifications are entirely reactive

electron/main.ts's notification system (built as the "R2: silent stall" fix) covers send failures, new replies, bounces, and corrupted state — all good, all necessary. But it's 100% error-driven. There is no proactive signal for the healthy-but-idle case: a user who set up an account, sent once, and never came back gets no nudge at all.

The premium AI tier is invisible

Because entitlement enforcement isn't wired, a paying-tier feature (the first-class local AI model) is presented identically to the free one. There's no upgrade prompt, no "you're on the free model" indicator, no comparison — the product has a monetizable feature that currently looks like a free one to every user.

Dead CTA on the pricing page

The landing site's "Business & Teams" tier is a waitlist-only "Coming soon" block. That's a legitimate signal-capture pattern if intentional — but paired with the also-broken Solo-tier checkout, the pricing page currently cannot sell anything at any tier.

Feature Opportunities

The existing feature set is broad: multi-account SMTP + Graph, campaigns, drip sequences with auto-stop-on-reply, scheduler, shared suppression, AI-classified reply detection, domain-auth advisory, and a fully local AI assistant. The gaps are less "missing features" and more "existing engine, no packaging around it."

Near-term

  • "Inbox-Safe" status card (R6) — the app already computes domain auth, suppression, and bounce data; it has never been surfaced as a single trust signal.
  • Sequence health nudges — the scheduler/sequence runner already knows when a sequence has stalled; nothing tells the user.
  • Reply-rate benchmarking (R11) — statsManager.ts already tracks total sent and reply rate; showing it against a category baseline turns a passive number into a habit-forming signal.

Bold bets — 3–5 year horizon

  • Agentic sequence planning. The on-device AI already drafts and classifies; extending it to plan a full sequence from a one-line goal ("re-engage last quarter's leads who went cold") entirely on-device is a capability cloud-based competitors (Instantly, Smartlead, Lemlist) structurally can't match without breaking their own privacy story.
  • Free public "Inbox-Safe Checker." Expose a slice of the existing domain-auth engine as a free single-domain SPF/DKIM/DMARC checker on the landing page — a classic PLG top-of-funnel move (Mailchimp/Postmark do this), and it lands directly on the 2026 Gmail/Yahoo compliance search-intent spike found in research (§12).
  • Thin browser companion. A read-mostly Gmail/Outlook-web extension that surfaces AI draft/personalize inline where people already write, funneling scheduled/sequence work back into the desktop app — expands reach without abandoning the local-first architecture (heavy lifting stays on-device).
  • Follow-up playbook exchange. A Notion-style template gallery for sequence structures — framed strictly as "follow-up playbooks," never "cold email scripts," to stay inside the positioning invariant.

Technical Improvements

  • Structured error/crash reporting. No crash-reporting or structured error telemetry exists beyond the reactive notification system — worth adding as a companion to R3's funnel telemetry (same local-first, opt-in principle).
  • Licensing/entitlement layer. R1's webhook and R2's tier gating need a small, real entitlement store (a signed license flag, not a network call on every launch) — this is genuinely new infrastructure, not just wiring.

Trend Research Findings

Method note

The content-trend-researcher skill referenced in the brief is a generic SEO/content-outline tool with no live API access (per its own documented limitations) — invoking it produces a reasoning framework, not real numbers. Its intent-analysis/content-gap structure informed the framing of §7's content strategy; the figures below come from live web research instead.

Deliverability rules are tightening — and the product already complies

Any domain sending 5,000+ messages/day to Gmail is now a classified "bulk sender" and must have SPF, DKIM, and DMARC alignment, sub-0.3% spam rate (0.1% recommended), and one-click unsubscribe. Compliant senders see ~89% inbox placement vs. 22–34% for non-compliant senders — a 3–7× penalty. Email Pilots' existing suppression/bounce/domain-auth engine already targets this bar; it's currently invisible to users (R6) and unmarketed (R12).

AI personalization is the category's current arms race

Vendor-reported figures (treat as directional, self-interested marketing claims, not independent data): generic cold email ~1–2% reply rate vs. AI-personalized 15–18%; AI-assisted campaigns report 35% higher conversions and 41% higher click-through. Every major competitor (Instantly, Smartlead, Lemlist) now leans on AI personalization as a headline feature — all via cloud API calls.

On-device/local AI is a genuine, on-trend differentiator

2026 commentary frames on-device AI as "not a legal checkbox — a conversion lever," with local-first architecture becoming the standard pattern for privacy-bound, latency-sensitive features. Email Pilots' AI assistant is already fully local (zero cloud calls) — a real structural edge over every named competitor above, currently unmentioned in landing copy (R7).

Referrals are outperforming cold outbound in B2B SaaS

Response rates on cold outbound have dropped sharply industry-wide as AI-generated sales email has flooded inboxes; referrals and word-of-mouth have re-emerged as the more credible channel, with top-performing SaaS companies relying 2–3× more on advocacy/referral than peers. Category-adjacent tools (Saleshandy, Reply.io) already run affiliate programs at 25% commission (R10).

SaaS pricing psychology, 2026 benchmarks

3-tier pricing with a decoy option shows ~1.4× conversion lift over flat pricing. Median trial-to-paid conversion across 200 products was 8% overall; opt-in trials converted at 8.9%, card-required trials at 31.4%.

Risks and Trade-offs

  • Sequencing risk (R1 before R2/R4). Shipping checkout before entitlement enforcement means briefly selling a plan whose premium value isn't gated. Acceptable — revenue collection matters more than a few weeks of an under-differentiated tier — but R2 should follow within days, not months.
  • Billing-model risk (R5, R14). The current "$2.9/wk, no card required" is a deliberately low-friction, low-anxiety hook and a stated trust signal. Overcorrecting toward card-required/annual-first could suppress top-of-funnel trial starts even while raising per-trial conversion. Treat as an A/B, not a wholesale replacement.
  • Telemetry risk (R3). Must stay strictly local/opt-in/aggregate. A conventional third-party analytics SDK piping data off-device would directly contradict the privacy-first positioning this report recommends building a whole marketing angle around (R7) — and conflicts with CLAUDE.md's broader "secrets never leave the device" posture.
  • Positioning risk (R10, R12). This product has a standing invariant: never market as "cold email" — payment-processor ban risk. Affiliate partners and content marketing both carry a real risk of drifting into that framing if not tightly briefed.
  • Deliverability-claims risk (R6, R12). Any public inbox-placement claims must hold up at real usage volume, not just today's low-volume pattern — verify under load before publishing hard numbers.
  • Notification-fatigue risk (R9). The existing reactive notification system was deliberately built with throttling to avoid nagging (memory: prior "silent stall" fix was calibrated carefully). A poorly tuned proactive nudge undercuts that calm, trustworthy tone.
  • Scope risk (R13 full build). Building a real multi-seat Teams tier before Solo-tier revenue is proven risks scope creep on unproven demand — keep the waitlist, don't build until Q1–Q2 signal justifies it.
